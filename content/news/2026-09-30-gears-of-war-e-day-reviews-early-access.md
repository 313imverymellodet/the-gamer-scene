The most important Xbox game of 2026 is days away from launch, and the first real verdicts land tomorrow morning.

**Gears of War: E-Day** launches **October 6** on **Xbox Series X|S and PC**, and it is on **Game Pass on day one**. Reviews go live on **Thursday, October 1 at 6 a.m. PT / 9 a.m. ET / 2 p.m. BST**, five days before launch. Early access begins on **October 2**.

That schedule matters. A five-day gap between reviews and release is a confident move from a publisher: Microsoft is giving critics time to shape the conversation before most players can buy the game. It is the opposite of the day-of-launch embargo that usually signals nerves.

## What E-Day actually is

E-Day is a prequel set on **Emergence Day**, the night the Locust Horde broke through the surface of Sera and the war that defines the series began. It puts players back in the boots of a young **Marcus Fenix** and **Dom Santiago**, years before the original 2006 game.

The Coalition built the game in **Unreal Engine 5**, and the pitch leans hard on the things the series has always sold:

- **Expanded destruction**, with environments that break apart under fire
- **Dynamic environmental damage** that changes cover and sightlines during fights
- **Heavier gore effects** — this is a Gears game, after all

After years of the franchise leaning toward spectacle and scale, E-Day's marketing has emphasized horror: the Locust as something unknown, coming out of the ground, rather than a familiar enemy army.

## Co-op is back where it belongs

Gears has always been at its best as a two-player game on a couch, and E-Day keeps that alive:

- **Campaign:** four-player online co-op and **two-player split-screen** on console
- **Versus:** 4v4 competitive multiplayer
- **Horde Siege:** a new **12-player cooperative mode**, the biggest co-op format the series has tried

Horde Siege is the feature to watch. Horde mode defined Gears multiplayer for a generation, and scaling it to twelve players could either be chaotic fun or a mess that loses what made the original mode tense. Early reviews should tell us which.

## Early access vs. launch day

If you want to play before October 6, check your edition. Early access starts October 2 on Xbox and PC; check the store listing to confirm whether your version includes it before you buy an upgrade on the assumption.

If you are on Game Pass, the math is simple: the game is included on day one, October 6.

## Why this launch matters for Xbox

E-Day is carrying more weight than any single game should. Xbox has spent the last two years shipping first-party games to PlayStation and Switch 2, reworking Game Pass pricing, and rebuilding its identity around "Xbox everywhere." Gears is one of the few franchises that still reads as *Xbox's* — and E-Day is the first mainline entry since Gears 5 in 2019.

It also has a narrow window. **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4** arrives October 23, and **GTA 6** follows November 19. E-Day has roughly two and a half weeks to become the game everybody is talking about before the calendar moves on. ## What reviews need to answer

When the embargo lifts tomorrow, these are the questions we will be reading for:

1. **Is the campaign a real reinvention, or Gears 5 with a younger Marcus?** The horror framing is a promise. The campaign has to deliver it.
2. **Does Horde Siege work at 12 players?** Bigger is not automatically better.
3. **How is PC performance?** Unreal Engine 5 games have had a rough few years on PC. Stutter at launch would hurt word of mouth.
4. **Is multiplayer built to last?** A Gears launch lives or dies on whether versus and Horde still have a community in month three.

## The Gamer Scene take

E-Day does not need to be the best game of the year. It needs to remind people why Gears mattered in the first place: tight cover shooting, chunky co-op, and a sense that the Locust are *terrifying*.

If the reviews land well tomorrow, Xbox has the October it needs. If they are mixed, the story becomes the Xbox brand again. We will have our full breakdown after the embargo lifts.
