The estimates are over. On November 20, Rockstar Games published the first official sales figures for **Grand Theft Auto VI**, and they're bigger than almost anyone predicted.

According to [Take-Two's announcement](https://www.take2games.com/ir/news/grand-theft-auto-vi-shatters-entertainment-box-office-records), the game generated **$3.79 billion in day-one retail sales** and sold **32.5 million units before release**. Rockstar calls the first number a record for entertainment "across any medium," and [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-20/-grand-theft-auto-vi-sells-3-79-billion-making-it-biggest-entertainment-launch-ever) described it as the biggest entertainment launch ever.

Sam Houser, Rockstar's president and founder, kept his statement short: "We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world."

## What the $3.79 billion actually measures

The headline number comes with a footnote, and it matters. Rockstar says the figure is **based on company estimates through the end of the day on November 19, worldwide**.

Three things follow from that:

- **It's an estimate.** It's Rockstar's own estimate, not an audited financial result.
- **It's retail spending, not Take-Two's revenue.** This is what players paid at stores and digital storefronts. Sony, Microsoft and retailers take their share before Take-Two books anything.
- **The release doesn't say how preorders are counted.** With 32.5 million copies sold before launch, preorders are clearly a large part of the story. The announcement doesn't separate them from launch-day purchases.

So it's fair to say GTA 6 had the biggest launch in entertainment history. It isn't fair to say Take-Two made $3.79 billion in a day.

## How it compares with the estimates

Before launch, the best available numbers came from analysts. On November 17, [Sensor Tower estimated](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders) nearly 16 million copies and more than $1.5 billion in sales. That was already a startling figure.

Rockstar's official pre-release number is roughly double the unit estimate. The gap isn't necessarily a sign that Sensor Tower was wrong. Third-party trackers usually model digital storefront sales, while Rockstar's figure covers everything, including retail boxes and its own store. But it's a reminder that the outside estimates we all relied on during the run-up were incomplete.

## How it compares with GTA V

When GTA V launched in 2013, Take-Two announced about $800 million in first-day sales and passed $1 billion within three days, a record at the time. GTA 6 has beaten that by a wide margin, even allowing for higher prices. The Standard Edition costs $79.99 and the Ultimate Edition $99.99, compared with $59.99 for GTA V at launch.

The new release also updates the franchise's long-term numbers. Rockstar says GTA V has now sold in nearly **235 million units**, and the series as a whole is at nearly **480 million**.

## What isn't included yet

The launch total covers only the PS5 and Xbox Series X|S versions. Two big pieces of the business are still to come:

- **PC.** Rockstar hasn't announced a PC release date. GamesBeat has [reported a Q1 2027 target](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-pc-early-2027-report), which Rockstar hasn't confirmed.
- **Online.** Rockstar hasn't announced when GTA 6's online mode will launch. The original [GTA Online keeps running](/news/2026-11-15-gta-online-after-gta-6) in the meantime.

For GTA V, both of those added years of revenue after launch. If the same pattern holds, the day-one total is only the opening chapter.

## Why it matters

For players, the number explains why every studio will be studying Rockstar's playbook for years: a single, enormous, delayed release, priced at $80, sold without a disc in the box and still breaking records.

For the industry, it lands in an awkward week. Take-Two's 2K label laid off around a third of [Civilization studio Firaxis](/news/2026-11-23-firaxis-layoffs-third-of-staff) two days before launch. Record revenue and layoffs at the same company are not a contradiction in modern games publishing, but the timing will be hard for many people to ignore.
