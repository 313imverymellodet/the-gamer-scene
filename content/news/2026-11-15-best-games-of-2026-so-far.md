Grand Theft Auto VI is about to dominate every conversation in gaming, so this is the perfect moment to look back at what 2026 delivered before Leonida arrived. It has been a remarkable year, with a roguelite masterpiece on new platforms, Housemarque at its peak, and a long-awaited Capcom new IP that was worth the wait.

Here are the year's best games, ranked by our own hands-on review scores, plus a couple of standouts we covered without a scored review.

## 1. Hades II — 9.5

Supergiant's sequel arrived on PlayStation and Xbox in its definitive form, and it's simply one of the best games ever made. The combat loop is endlessly replayable, the writing is sharp and the 120fps console version feels incredible. If you only buy one non-GTA game this year, make it this one.

[Read our Hades II review →](/reviews/hades-2)

## 2. Saros — 9.2

Housemarque took the bullet-hell roguelite formula it pioneered with Returnal and made it more accessible, more rewarding and more spectacular. The story doesn't always land, but the action is among the finest of this generation. It's the best PS5 exclusive of 2026.

[Read our Saros review →](/reviews/saros)

## 3. Pragmata — 9.2

Six years of delays, and Capcom delivered. Pragmata is one of the best new IPs in years, a heartfelt, mechanically original action game that made the wait feel worthwhile.

[Read our Pragmata review →](/reviews/pragmata)

## 4. Mouse: P.I. For Hire — 8.4

The year's best art direction. Its rubber-hose cartoon style earned every headline, and the surprise is that the shooting underneath actually delivers too.

[Read our Mouse: P.I. For Hire review →](/reviews/mouse-pi-for-hire)

## 5. Invincible VS — 7.8

A better fighting game than its licensed origins had any right to produce. Deep tag mechanics and a roster that feels authentic make up for a thin story mode, and online is where it really shines.

[Read our Invincible VS review →](/reviews/invincible-vs)

## Also worth your time

**Mixtape** was the critics' darling of 2026, with an OpenCritic score of 94 for a four-hour coming-of-age story. We didn't score it, but we argued it ended the "short games aren't worth it" debate for good. [Read the column →](/opinion/2026-05-12-short-games-are-not-a-problem)

**Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced** rebuilt the best pirate game ever made with gorgeous results and some modern Ubisoft baggage. Critic consensus sat at 85/100 on OpenCritic when we wrote our research-based [Review Analysis](/reviews/assassins-creed-black-flag-resynced-review-analysis).

## How we rank

This list follows our hands-on review scores. Games we covered through research-based Review Analyses aren't given a Gamer Scene score and aren't ranked against games we played ourselves. Every scored review lives in our [Review Index](/reviews).

## What 2026 got right

The common thread across this list is confidence. Hades II and Saros are roguelites built by studios that know exactly what they do best and refined it instead of chasing trends. Pragmata proved a big publisher will still bet on a strange new single-player idea. Mouse: P.I. For Hire showed that art direction can be the headline without the game underneath being an afterthought. And Mixtape reminded everyone that four great hours beat forty forgettable ones. It's been a year for focused, well-made games, which is a healthy sign heading into the biggest launch of the decade.
