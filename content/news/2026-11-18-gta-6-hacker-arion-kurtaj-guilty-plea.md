Four years after the leak that gave the world its first unauthorized look at **Grand Theft Auto VI**, the case has reached a turning point just as the game arrives. **Arion Kurtaj**, now 21, [pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court in London on November 16](https://kotaku.com/grand-theft-auto-6-leak-gta6-hacker-pleads-guilty-2000757500), according to Kotaku, citing the BBC.

The plea covers the hack of Rockstar's systems and other cybercrimes connected to the Lapsus$ hacking group.

## What happened in 2022

In September 2022, around 90 videos from an early build of GTA 6 appeared online. They showed unfinished animations, debug tools, placeholder assets and the first glimpse of a game Rockstar hadn't yet revealed. At that point, the studio had barely said anything publicly about the project.

Rockstar confirmed the intrusion at the time. The leak was one of the largest in the history of the industry, and it gave everyone from fans to rival developers an unplanned look inside a production that was still years from release.

Kotaku reports that Kurtaj, who was 17 at the time of the hack, carried it out while on bail for other offenses, and posted inside Rockstar's internal messaging system before threatening the company.

## The cost to Rockstar

[PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/grand-theft-auto-vi/hacker-guilty) and other outlets have described Rockstar's losses at around $5 million, along with thousands of staff hours spent dealing with the fallout. Coverage has also linked the breach to tighter security at the studio, including restrictions on remote work.

## When is sentencing?

Reports differ. Some coverage gives **November 23**, while Kotaku says sentencing will be scheduled for a later date. We'll update this article when the court's date is clear. As with any active case, the details could change, and we're sticking to what has been reported from the proceedings.

## This is not the CyberLeek case

It's easy to confuse the two, so here's the distinction:

| | 2022 leak | 2026 leaks |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Arion Kurtaj | "CyberLeek" |
| Status | Guilty plea, sentencing pending | Identity not public |
| Material | About 90 early development clips | Clips, an alleged map and possible build access |

Kotaku is explicit that CyberLeek is a separate incident. [Other reporting](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/gta-6-hacker-behind-original-150423830.html) describes that source as still active. There's been no public announcement of an arrest connected to the 2026 leaks. Our [earlier investigation of CyberLeek](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-leak-crisis-cyberleek) explains how those leaks unfolded and why you shouldn't click on anything tied to them.

## A strange final chapter before launch

The timing is almost cinematic. Rockstar's game has reached a [99 on Metacritic](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-reviews-metacritic-99-roundup) and an estimated [$1.5 billion in presales](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders), at the same moment the person whose breach exposed its earliest footage admits guilt in court.

What's striking in hindsight is how little the leak hurt commercially. The footage was unfinished and widely mocked by some viewers at the time, with developers across the industry defending what unfinished games actually look like. Four years later, the finished product is being called one of the most impressive ever made. If you want one example of why early development footage tells you very little about a final game, this is it.

The human cost is harder to measure. Developers had work they'd spent years on exposed before it was ready, and security changes followed. That's worth remembering every time a new "leak" goes viral.
