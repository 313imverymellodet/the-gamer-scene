A week after releasing the biggest entertainment launch in history, Rockstar Games' UK staff have voted **against union recognition**.

The ballot, which ran from **November 12 to November 26**, asked UK-based Rockstar developers whether to accept a recognition agreement with the **IWGB Game Workers Union**. According to [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/rockstar-games-union-vote-fails-as-75-percent-of-devs-vote-no-2001000621), about **75%** voted no.

[dlcompare](https://www.dlcompare.com/gaming-news/rockstar-workers-vote-75-against-union-recognition-after-gta-6-launch) reports the full count:

| | Votes | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Against recognition | 1,163 | 75.3% |
| For recognition | 381 | 24.7% |
| **Total** | **1,544** | Turnout 93.2% |

That's a decisive result on very high turnout. It isn't close enough to argue about.

## What a yes vote would have meant

Recognition would have formalized **collective bargaining** between Rockstar and the union. According to Kotaku, that would have covered issues including **overtime, bonuses, layoffs and AI**, with regular meetings between management and union representatives. dlcompare describes the scope as pay and working conditions.

Without recognition, those regular meetings won't happen. Rockstar employees can still join the union, but the company has no obligation to bargain with it.

## What Rockstar said

Jennifer Kolbe, Rockstar's head of publishing, told Kotaku the company is "extraordinarily proud of our team," and said the result reflects strong support for continuing Rockstar's direct relationships with its staff.

## What the union said

The IWGB struck a defiant tone. "This isn't a sign to retreat, it's a sign to double down on what we stand for," the union said, according to Kotaku. It called the recognition deal one route of many and said its priorities still include pay transparency, contractual overtime pay and protections against AI and layoffs.

## How we got here

The vote can't be separated from the dispute that started it.

In **October 2025**, Rockstar fired more than 30 UK employees. The company says they were dismissed for leaking confidential information about unannounced games. The workers and the IWGB say they were members of a private union Discord channel and call the firings union busting, which Rockstar denies.

The case went to an employment tribunal, and as we reported at launch, [hearings ended without a verdict](/news/2026-11-18-rockstar-union-tribunal-ends-gta-6-launch). According to dlcompare, closing submissions were heard in mid-November, and the **ruling is still pending**.

In June 2026, the Rockstar Game Workers Union, a branch of the IWGB, launched its push for recognition. Rockstar agreed in July to meet the union, which led to this ballot.

## What the vote doesn't settle

It's tempting to read this as a verdict on everything that came before. It isn't.

- **It doesn't decide the tribunal.** Whether Rockstar's firings were lawful is a separate legal question. A judge will decide it, not a staff vote.
- **It doesn't mean Rockstar staff have no concerns.** It means most of them didn't want this particular recognition agreement. People vote no for many reasons, including trust in management, worries about the union or simply timing.
- **It doesn't end organizing at Rockstar.** The IWGB has made clear it isn't going away.

The timing is also worth noting. Voting ran across the exact two weeks when Rockstar staff were shipping the most anticipated game of the decade, followed by [$3.79 billion in day-one sales](/news/2026-11-23-gta-6-sales-3-79-billion-biggest-launch-ever). It's hard to imagine a moment when confidence in the company would be higher.

## Why it matters

The IWGB said recognition would have made it the biggest recognized union in the UK games industry. Its failure is a setback for organizing across the sector, where layoffs have hit studios including [Firaxis](/news/2026-11-23-firaxis-layoffs-third-of-staff) and [Ubisoft](/news/2026-11-23-ubisoft-166-layoffs-quebec-sales-fall) this month alone.

But a 93% turnout also shows how seriously Rockstar's staff took the question. We've written more about that in [an opinion column](/opinion/2026-11-28-rockstar-union-vote-clear-answer).
