**Ubisoft** has cut **166 jobs** across its studios in **Montreal and Quebec City**, the company confirmed on November 19, according to [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/ubiosft-lays-off-166-in-canada). Hours later, it reported a sharp fall in sales for the first half of its financial year.

Ubisoft said the cuts reflect "ever-changing production needs." In its statement, the company said, "We deeply value the contributions of these teams," and that affected employees will receive severance, extended health benefits and career transition services.

## Who was affected

According to GamesIndustry.biz, as cited by Game Developer, the layoffs were mainly in **production and support roles**. Some affected employees work for **Vantage Studios**, the Ubisoft and Tencent joint venture that houses Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six.

Reports put the split at about **128 positions in Montreal and 38 in Quebec City**, which is reportedly less than 4% of Ubisoft's Canadian workforce. GamesIndustry.biz also reported that the cuts follow cancelled or significantly rescoped projects. Ubisoft hasn't said which ones.

It's the latest in a long run of cuts. In 2026 alone, Ubisoft has closed its Halifax studio in January, cut about 40 jobs at Ubisoft Toronto in February, and shut Ubisoft Winnipeg and Ubisoft Belgrade in June.

## The numbers

Ubisoft's first-half results, covering April through September, were weak overall, according to [Insider Gaming's summary of the earnings release](https://insider-gaming.com/ubisoft-reports-254-million-loss-net-bookings-fall/):

- **Net bookings:** €644.7 million, down **16.5%** from €772.4 million a year earlier
- **IFRS operating loss:** €189.2 million, wider than last year's €120.2 million
- **Back catalog:** down 40.8% to €438.8 million
- **Digital net bookings:** down 20.1% to €548.2 million

## The bright spot

The second quarter actually beat expectations. Net bookings for July through September came in at about **€389 million**, above Ubisoft's guidance of around €370 million.

Ubisoft gave much of the credit to **Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced**, the remake of the beloved 2013 pirate game. It sold more than 3 million copies in its first week in July and reached 3.5 million within two weeks, beating Ubisoft's expectations for the full year. Ubisoft also pointed to in-game spending, a bigger share of premium editions and more targeted marketing.

## What Ubisoft expects next

According to Insider Gaming, Ubisoft expects:

- Full-year net bookings to fall by a high single-digit percentage
- About **€250 million** in net bookings for October through December
- To return to positive free cash flow and operating income in its next financial year

The October-to-December quarter is a hard one to call, because it overlaps almost exactly with the launch of [Grand Theft Auto VI](/news/2026-11-23-gta-6-sales-3-79-billion-biggest-launch-ever). Few publishers wanted to release anything big this fall.

## Why it matters

Ubisoft's results tell a familiar 2026 story. A well-made remake of a game people already loved did better than expected, while the wider catalog and newer bets struggled. The company is clearly leaning into Assassin's Creed and its other biggest franchises through the Tencent-backed Vantage Studios, and cutting elsewhere.

For players, the bigger concern is the cancelled or rescoped projects behind these layoffs. Ubisoft hasn't named them, which means we may not know what was lost until the next showcase passes without them.
