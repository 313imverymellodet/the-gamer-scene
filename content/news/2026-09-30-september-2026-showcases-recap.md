September is usually the month the industry sets up the holidays. This year it did more than that: in a span of two weeks, Sony and Nintendo mapped out their release slates well into 2027, and Sony's biggest exclusive of the fall arrived to a much cooler reception than anyone expected.

If you missed any of it, here is what matters.

## State of Play, September 3: the Final Fantasy VII finale is dated

Sony's **double-length State of Play** featured more than 30 games. The show ended with the reveal everyone was waiting for.

### Final Fantasy VII Revelation — April 8, 2027

The third and final chapter of Square Enix's remake project launches **April 8, 2027**, and it is **not a PlayStation exclusive**. Revelation launches the same day on **PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2 and PC** via Steam and the Epic Games Store.

The roughly 10-minute gameplay segment focused on scale: open-world exploration aboard the **Highwind** airship, chocobo travel, and grappling traversal. A **Trilogy Edition** preorder includes immediate access to *Final Fantasy VII Remake Intergrade* and *Final Fantasy VII Rebirth*, and a digital **Premium Edition** includes the Story Expansion Pass and 24 hours of early access.

That makes the entire remake trilogy available across all current platforms, a complete reversal from the PlayStation exclusivity that defined the first two games.

### The rest of the show

- **Marvel's Wolverine** got one final look ahead of its September 15 launch (see below)
- **Ghost of Yōtei** gets a **Complete Edition on October 1** and a new run-based bounty mode, **Most Wanted**, with several playable characters — including a returning **Jin Sakai**
- **Until Dawn 2** and **Metro 2039** received release dates
- **Maneater 2** was revealed as a surprise sequel
- **Crimson Desert** got a new expansion, **Gran Turismo 7** got a major update, and Sony showed **two limited-edition GTA 6 DualSense controllers**

## Marvel's Wolverine: Insomniac's lowest-rated original game in a decade

**Marvel's Wolverine** launched on **PS5 on September 15**, after reviews went live on September 10. It sits at **78 on Metacritic** from more than 100 critic reviews, and **78 on OpenCritic** with 73% of critics recommending it.

That is a solid score for most games. For Insomniac, it is a disappointment. *Marvel's Spider-Man* scored 87, *Miles Morales* 85, and *Spider-Man 2* 90. Wolverine is the studio's lowest-rated original release in about a decade.

The reviews follow a clear pattern:

- **Praised:** Liam McIntyre's performance as Logan, and the game's visual presentation
- **Criticized:** a more linear structure, thin enemy variety, and a story that critics say undersells its own premise

The user score has also become a battleground, with Metacritic user reviews flooded by both zero and perfect scores in the days after launch. We are ignoring that noise.

## Nintendo Direct, September 9: Switch 2's 2027 takes shape

Nintendo followed a Zelda 40th anniversary presentation on September 8 with a roughly **45-minute general Direct** on September 9. Every game shown was for Switch 2.

### The headliners

- **Metroid Ravenous** — a brand-new **2D Metroid**, launching **January 28, 2027**, exclusively on Switch 2
- **Kirby and the World Beyond** — a **3D open-world Kirby** adventure built around copy abilities, coming **next spring** as a Switch 2 exclusive, timed to the series' 35th anniversary
- **Professor Layton and the New World of Steam** — finally dated for **December 10** on Switch 2 and Switch
- **Monster Hunter Wilds: Ascendance** — an expansion headed to Switch 2 in 2027

Putting a new 2D Metroid in late January, right as the holiday rush ends, is a strong signal. Nintendo is not front-loading everything into 2026; it is building a steady cadence into next year.

## The Gamer Scene take

The most important trend in September was not any single game. It was that **exclusivity keeps eroding**. Final Fantasy VII Revelation launches everywhere on day one. Call of Duty ships on Switch 2. Kojima's next game has moved from PlayStation to Xbox.

The platforms that still have true exclusives — Nintendo with Metroid and Kirby, Sony with Wolverine — are being judged more harshly on them. Wolverine's 78 would be a win for most studios. For a first-party PlayStation tentpole in 2026, it is a warning that "good" is no longer enough to carry a platform's fall.
