Another PlayStation live-service game is gone. **Fairgames**, the online heist shooter from Montreal's **Haven Studios**, has been cancelled, according to a [Bloomberg report covered by Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/console/report-playstation-s-fairgame-cancelled-but-haven-studios-remains).

The rare good news: **nobody is being laid off.** According to a Sony spokesperson, as reported by Game Developer, Haven will instead move into a **support role** for other PlayStation studios.

## What was Fairgames?

Fairgames, stylized as FAIRGAME$, was revealed at a **PlayStation Showcase in May 2023**. It was pitched as a competitive heist game in which teams of players rob the world's wealthiest people. It was Haven's only project.

Haven was founded in 2021 by veteran producer **Jade Raymond**, best known for her work on early Assassin's Creed games, alongside creative director **Mathieu Leduc**. Sony bought the studio in 2022.

The cancellation came after more than three years of near silence. Before this month, the last major Fairgames news was Raymond's reported departure from the studio in 2025.

## Why it was cancelled

Sony hasn't laid out a detailed reason. Reports have said the game's playtests fell short of targets, and one widely cited Sony message reportedly pointed to "challenging market conditions" in the player-versus-player shooter genre.

Whatever the internal details, the wider context is clear. Competitive shooters are one of the hardest markets in gaming. Players already have years invested in Fortnite, Call of Duty, Apex Legends and others, and new games struggle to pull them away.

## A pattern for PlayStation

Fairgames joins a long list of PlayStation live-service projects that didn't make it:

- **Concord** launched in 2024 and was shut down within weeks.
- Sony's **London Studio** was closed in 2024.
- Several other live-service projects were cancelled before they were ever shown.

A few years ago, Sony said it wanted to release a large slate of live-service games. That plan has been cut back sharply. The biggest exception has been **Helldivers 2**, a huge hit, and even that came from an outside studio, Arrowhead.

## What happens to Haven?

According to the Sony spokesperson's comments, Haven will collaborate with other teams across PlayStation Studios while exploring new creative opportunities. Sony hasn't said which games it will help with.

Support roles can be a lifeline or a slow decline, depending on what follows. Some studios use them to build skills and pitch new projects. Others are quietly folded into bigger teams. Haven's future depends on whether Sony gives it another game of its own.

## Why it matters

For players, losing an unreleased game you've seen only in a trailer isn't a huge loss. For the industry, the important part is how it was handled. Cancellations usually mean layoffs, as Bandai Namco's [closure of Reflector Entertainment](/news/2026-11-28-bandai-namco-closing-reflector-unknown-9) this month shows. Keeping a whole studio employed after cancelling its only game is unusual, and it's the right call.

It also shows where PlayStation's priorities are now: fewer live-service bets, and more focus on big single-player games like *Ghost of Yōtei*, *Saros* and *Marvel's Wolverine*.
