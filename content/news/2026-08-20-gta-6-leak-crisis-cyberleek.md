> **Spoiler warning:** This article discusses broad gameplay systems reportedly visible in leaked footage. It does not reproduce the videos, leaked map images or potential story spoilers, and it does not link to any of the material.

Rockstar Games' carefully controlled rollout for **Grand Theft Auto VI** has been disrupted by what appears to be the game's most significant leak since the massive security breach of 2022.

What began with a pair of short gameplay clips has expanded into multiple videos, a purported map of Leonida and a new clip that may indicate the person or group behind the leaks has direct access to a playable development build.

The footage has been attributed to an online source calling itself **CyberLeek**. Rockstar and parent company Take-Two Interactive have not publicly authenticated the material, but numerous reposts have been removed following copyright claims. The speed of those takedowns has strengthened the belief that at least some of the footage is genuine.

## The leaks started with basketball and road rage

One of the first widely circulated clips reportedly shows protagonist **Jason Duval** outside the worn-down waterfront property previously seen in Rockstar's official marketing.

Jason picks up a basketball and begins shooting at a hoop. Making a basket appears to increase a character statistic labeled "Focus" by two percent. The footage also shows two separate money totals in the interface, potentially representing cash on hand and money stored elsewhere.

The clip suggests basketball could be one of several optional activities available throughout Leonida. However, the exact purpose of the Focus statistic remains unknown. Comparisons have already been made to character progression and slow-motion targeting systems from Rockstar's Red Dead Redemption games, but that interpretation has not been confirmed.

A second clip reportedly shows Jason driving through Leonida before colliding with a delivery truck. The incident escalates into a fight with the driver, eventually attracting police attention.

Several possible gameplay systems are visible during the encounter:

- A wanted level that appears capable of reaching six stars.
- A stamina meter that decreases during prolonged combat.
- A police-awareness indicator resembling a stealth detection meter.
- Icons suggesting police can identify Jason by his clothing, appearance and vehicle.
- A negative symbol that may represent an honor, morality or reputation system.
- Vehicle information for fuel and engine condition.
- Trunk options labeled for storage and loadout management.

These details could point toward a more simulation-heavy crime system in which changing clothes, abandoning a known vehicle or breaking the police line of sight matters more than it did in GTA V. They could also be unfinished experiments that have since been altered or removed.

## More clips reveal vehicle theft, tasers and NPC interactions

Additional footage reportedly shows Jason entering a secured distribution facility and confronting guards.

When approaching a locked vehicle, the player is presented with options resembling "Smash Window" and "Clone Key." That could mean stealing cars will involve different approaches depending on the vehicle, available equipment or how quietly the player wants to operate.

The same footage reportedly includes guards using tasers, Jason taking a hat from a downed NPC, the theft of a semi-truck and a short scene involving Jason's conspiracy-obsessed friend **Cal Hampton**. Other clips appear to show additional melee weapons, vehicle theft interactions, pain medication and options for responding to NPCs.

None of these systems should be considered final until Rockstar demonstrates them officially. Development builds commonly contain temporary interfaces, test mechanics and features that may not survive production.

## The latest clip suggests the leaker can control the game

The most concerning development arrived on August 20.

The newest footage reportedly shows Jason flying a small plane over Leonida while cycling through different radio stations. After landing, Jason fires into a wall and uses the bullet holes to spell "LEEK."

That personalized action suggests the source is not simply uploading a folder of prerecorded testing videos. Whoever created the clip appears capable of controlling Jason and deliberately recording new material on request. The Verge reported that this could mean an actual playable copy or development build has escaped Rockstar's control.

That still does not prove CyberLeek possesses the complete retail game. The source could have access to a restricted testing environment, an incomplete development branch, a limited portion of the campaign or someone inside the development pipeline who can record requested footage. There is currently no verified information explaining how the material was obtained or how much of the game is accessible.

Even so, the ability to create new footage would make this situation considerably more serious than a one-time media leak.

## A purported full map of Leonida is circulating

Alongside the videos, CyberLeek has distributed images that allegedly show the complete in-game map of Leonida.

The images appear to outline Vice City, roads, islands, rural regions, points of interest and other locations across Rockstar's fictional version of Florida. One version may also show markers for Jason and Lucia in different areas of the world.

The map is relatively light on visual detail, leading to several possibilities: it could be an unfinished version, a simplified interface used during development, a community reconstruction or a mixture of legitimate and altered material.

Rockstar has not confirmed the map's authenticity or officially revealed the complete shape of Leonida. Fans hoping to explore the world without advance knowledge should be especially careful on social media, where cropped versions of the map are being reposted without spoiler warnings.

## The footage may be from a 2023 build

Although portions of the leaked footage look polished, there are reasons not to treat the clips as representative of the game players will receive in November.

The leaker has reportedly claimed that the gameplay comes from a 2023 development build. A separate industry source also described the footage as more than a year old. Neither claim has been independently verified.

Temporary-looking interface elements, unfinished textures and inconsistent visual quality also suggest the material may come from an older stage of development. Rockstar could have spent several years revising the controls, graphics, artificial intelligence and underlying gameplay systems shown in the clips.

This distinction is important. Leaked development footage is not the equivalent of a review build, a public demo or the finished product. A six-star wanted system, gas consumption, basketball, morality indicators and other visible mechanics may still be present at launch — but none should be presented as confirmed features yet.

## CyberLeek claims the leaks are a consumer-rights protest

CyberLeek has attached a manifesto to the leaks criticizing several practices in the video game industry.

The source's demands reportedly include ending digital preorders, opposing single-player content sold separately after already being included in game data and requiring offline fallback options so purchased single-player content remains usable after online services are discontinued.

CyberLeek has threatened to continue targeting publishers until those companies acknowledge the demands and commit to changing their practices. However, the campaign is also being used to promote a cryptocurrency.

The leaked videos and images reportedly contain crypto advertising, QR codes and links to unfamiliar websites. CyberLeek claims money raised through the cryptocurrency will support a secret project related to digital ownership and game preservation, but no independently verifiable information has been provided about that project or how the funds would be used.

That contradiction has caused widespread suspicion. A source presenting itself as a consumer advocate while using stolen material to advertise a speculative financial asset gives players several reasons to remain cautious.

> **Do not scan QR codes, download files, connect cryptocurrency wallets or send money to accounts associated with the leak.**

## Stop Killing Games condemns the leakers

The game-preservation campaign Stop Killing Games has rejected CyberLeek's tactics despite sharing some concerns about long-term access to purchased games.

The organization warned players not to send money to the source and argued that illegal leaks harm developers while damaging legitimate efforts to improve game preservation and consumer ownership.

Its response emphasized that decisions about distribution, downloadable content and physical media are generally made by company leadership — not the thousands of artists, programmers, designers, writers and testers whose unfinished work is being exposed.

That distinction matters. A leak may embarrass an executive team, but developers are often the people forced to deal with internal investigations, additional security restrictions, disrupted marketing plans and the emotional impact of seeing unfinished work judged publicly.

## The source has reportedly threatened bigger spoilers

CyberLeek has indicated that more material could be released, and one report says the source has even threatened to reveal the game's ending.

There is no proof that CyberLeek possesses GTA 6's ending or complete campaign. However, the apparent ability to produce new gameplay footage makes the threat more difficult to dismiss than an ordinary social-media rumor.

Players attempting to remain spoiler-free should consider muting phrases connected to GTA 6, Jason, Lucia, Leonida and CyberLeek across social platforms. Video thumbnails, suggested posts and reposted map images may reveal information even when the original accounts have been removed.

## Rockstar is fighting the spread with copyright claims

Rockstar and Take-Two have not issued a detailed public statement about CyberLeek as of August 20.

Instead, their most visible response has been a wave of copyright claims against accounts and posts sharing the material. Multiple clips viewed by reporters were later removed, while at least one social-media account said it had been locked after reposting footage.

Copyright enforcement does not officially confirm every claim surrounding the leak. A takedown establishes that the rights holder objects to unauthorized distribution; it does not verify fan interpretations of the interface, the alleged age of the build or the authenticity of every map circulating online.

Still, the sustained takedown campaign makes it increasingly likely that the core gameplay footage originated from Rockstar-controlled material.

## This is not GTA 6's first major security problem

In September 2022, approximately 90 videos from an unfinished version of GTA 6 were posted online in a roughly three-gigabyte collection. The footage exposed early development work, internal tools, placeholder assets, protagonist Lucia and an early version of Vice City.

Take-Two moved quickly to remove copies, and Rockstar later acknowledged that unauthorized material had been accessed.

Rockstar also confirmed a separate breach involving a third-party provider in April 2026. The company said the compromised information was limited and that the incident would not affect its organization or players. There is currently no publicly established connection between that third-party breach and the CyberLeek footage.

## Rockstar's official GTA 6 presentation is August 27

The timing of the leaks is particularly damaging because Rockstar is only days away from its largest official GTA 6 presentation yet.

**Grand Theft Auto VI: An Extended Look** will premiere for Netflix subscribers on **August 27 at 3 p.m. ET**. The presentation is scheduled to reach Rockstar's YouTube channel and GTA 6 website six hours later. The game remains officially scheduled for **November 19, 2026**, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

The presentation should give players a much better benchmark for evaluating the leaks. Rockstar's current footage will show how the game's graphics, interface and systems compare with the alleged 2023 material.

Major questions now include whether the official demonstration confirms:

- The six-star wanted level.
- Police identification based on clothing and vehicles.
- Basketball and other recreational activities.
- Character statistics such as Focus and stamina.
- Fuel, engine damage and vehicle storage.
- Expanded stealth and NPC-interaction systems.
- The overall scale and structure of Leonida.

## The Gamer Scene take

The leaked footage makes GTA 6 look ambitious, but individual gameplay discoveries are no longer the biggest part of this story.

The real concern is that CyberLeek may have continuing access to a playable version of the game. If that appearance is accurate, Rockstar could face a steady stream of unauthorized footage and potentially significant story spoilers through launch.

CyberLeek's stated concerns about preservation, offline access and digital ownership are part of a legitimate industry conversation. Attaching those concerns to stolen development material and a cryptocurrency promotion, however, makes the operation look less like organized consumer advocacy and more like an attempt to monetize attention surrounding the most anticipated game of the year.

For players, the smartest move is to avoid the source's links, treat every visible mechanic as unfinished and wait for Rockstar's August 27 presentation before drawing conclusions about the final game.

GTA 6 has spent years as one of gaming's most closely guarded projects. With three months remaining before release, keeping the rest of Leonida under wraps may now be Rockstar's toughest mission.
