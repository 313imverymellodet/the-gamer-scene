Two days before Take-Two released the biggest entertainment launch in history, it cut deep into one of its most respected studios.

**Firaxis Games**, the developer of **Civilization** and **XCOM**, laid off around **60 employees**, roughly **a third of its staff**, on November 17, according to [Bloomberg's Jason Schreier, as reported by Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2026/11/17/civilization-developer-firaxis-lays-off-a-third-of-its-staff). Studio head **Heather Hazen** is also leaving.

2K, the Take-Two label that owns Firaxis, didn't confirm the number. In a statement to Bloomberg, a spokesperson said the studio "is restructuring and recentering around the studio's core strength," and that its size is being reduced to focus on design, technology and the player experience.

## What's happening at Firaxis

According to Bloomberg, citing anonymous employees, the studio has been working on an **unannounced XCOM game** that has had a challenging development and recently missed an important milestone. Work on the game is reportedly continuing, but the layoffs could change it. If it ships, it would be Firaxis's first XCOM game since *XCOM: Chimera Squad* in 2020.

The studio's recent track record helps explain the pressure:

- **Civilization VII** launched in February 2025. Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick later described its sales as "slow," according to Game Informer, and it still sits at a "Mixed" user rating on Steam.
- **Marvel's Midnight Suns**, released in 2022, was well reviewed but reportedly underperformed commercially.

It isn't the studio's first round of cuts. Firaxis also lost staff in 2023, and 2K announced layoffs there again in 2025, [Windows Central reported](https://www.windowscentral.com/gaming/2k-quietly-announces-layoffs-at-civilization-7-dev-firaxis-amid-hollow-knight-silksong-launch-hype-but-wont-say-how-many).

## The timing

It's hard to separate this story from the calendar. Firaxis staff lost their jobs on Tuesday, November 17. On Thursday, November 19, Take-Two released **Grand Theft Auto VI**. On Friday, Rockstar announced [$3.79 billion in day-one retail sales](/news/2026-11-23-gta-6-sales-3-79-billion-biggest-launch-ever).

Those are different studios, different budgets and different decisions. Take-Two runs its labels separately, and a hit at Rockstar doesn't automatically pay for an under-performing strategy game at 2K. But to players and developers watching the same company celebrate a record while cutting a third of a beloved studio, the explanation won't feel like much comfort.

It also isn't the only labor story at Take-Two this month. In the UK, the [tribunal over 34 workers Rockstar dismissed](/news/2026-11-18-rockstar-union-tribunal-ends-gta-6-launch) ended without a verdict as the game launched. We've argued that you can [enjoy GTA 6 and still care about how those workers are treated](/opinion/2026-11-18-play-gta-6-care-about-fired-workers).

## What it means for Civilization and XCOM

**Civilization isn't going anywhere.** It's one of the longest-running and most valuable strategy franchises in gaming, and 2K's statement frames the restructuring as a return to Firaxis's core strengths, which is strategy.

**XCOM is the bigger question.** A project that reportedly missed a milestone, followed by heavy layoffs and a leadership change, is the kind of combination that usually leads to delays, a reduced scope or worse. Nothing has been announced, so for now that's analysis, not news. But XCOM fans should expect a long wait before they hear anything official.

## The bigger picture

Firaxis is part of a rough week for strategy and AAA developers. Ubisoft also announced [166 layoffs in Montreal and Quebec City](/news/2026-11-23-ubisoft-166-layoffs-quebec-sales-fall) on November 19. Across the industry, companies are cutting studios that haven't delivered hits while spending heavily on the few franchises that do.
