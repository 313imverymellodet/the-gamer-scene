As **Grand Theft Auto VI** rolls out worldwide, one of the most uncomfortable stories surrounding it remains unresolved. The employment tribunal in Glasgow over Rockstar's dismissal of workers has [concluded, with a final judgment still to come](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/rockstars-union-busting-tribunal-comes-to-an-end-pending-verdict/), according to Video Games Chronicle.

A reporter who attended the hearing estimated the decision could take anywhere from a few weeks to a few months. In the meantime, the workers can't return to their jobs.

## What the case is about

Rockstar dismissed **34 workers in 2025**, according to VGC. The dispute centers on why.

**The union's position.** The Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain (IWGB) calls the firings union busting, which it says is illegal under UK labor law. It argues that the workers' communications were protected, and it has accused Rockstar of spying on a union-related Discord server.

**Rockstar's position.** Rockstar denies the allegations. It says the workers were dismissed for leaking confidential information to people outside the company.

No court has yet ruled on the central question. Both accounts remain contested, and the outcome could go either way.

## What has already been decided

An earlier ruling went Rockstar's way on a narrower issue. [The tribunal declined to grant interim relief](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/new-ruling-in-rockstar-games-legal-battle-means-the-gta-6-studio-wont-be-forced-to-pay-fired-employees-interim-relief-we-regret-that-we-were-put-in-a-position-where-dismissals-were-necessary), the emergency measure that would have kept workers on pay until the full case was heard. That's not a final verdict. It means the workers didn't meet the high bar for immediate relief.

Workers and supporters have [rallied outside the Glasgow tribunal](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/-justice-at-last-feels-within-reach-fired-rockstar-workers-rally-in-glasgow-as-union-busting-tribunal-begins) and at Rockstar's Edinburgh office.

## Not a boycott

The most interesting part of the workers' campaign is what they've chosen *not* to ask for. As we [reported in August](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-news-roundup), the dismissed employees have told fans not to boycott GTA 6. Their argument is that they spent years helping build it and want people to experience that work, while also paying attention to how those workers were treated.

That's an unusually nuanced position at a moment when the conversation around this game is anything but nuanced.

## The context around the launch

The contrast is hard to miss. In the same week, GTA 6 reached a [99 on Metacritic](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-reviews-metacritic-99-roundup) and an estimated [$1.5 billion in presales](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders). NBC, citing The Guardian, reports a development cost above $1 billion and a team of nearly 5,000 people.

Rockstar has also been dealing with genuine security issues for years, including the [2022 hack](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-hacker-arion-kurtaj-guilty-plea) and this year's [CyberLeek leaks](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-leak-crisis-cyberleek). That's the backdrop to its argument about confidentiality. The union would say that security concerns can't be used as cover for punishing workers who organize. The tribunal now has to weigh those claims based on the evidence.

## Why this matters beyond Rockstar

Game development has a long-running conversation about crunch, job security and unionization. A ruling here could be closely watched across the industry, particularly in the UK, because it concerns how far workplace conversations are protected when they touch on confidential projects. That's a question plenty of studios will care about, whichever way the judgment lands.

## What happens next

The judgment will come when it comes, quite possibly after the launch excitement has faded. We'll report the outcome when it's published.
