Miami is turning into Vice City, and not everyone in Miami is happy about it.

Ahead of **Grand Theft Auto VI's** launch, Miami-Dade County struck a **$3.9 million advertising agreement** tied to the game. A giant "Welcome to Vice City" display sits on the roof of the county-owned Kaseya Center, and the campaign has drawn opposition from local law enforcement and elected officials.

One key fact has been getting lost: **according to Mayor Daniella Levine Cava's office, Rockstar is paying.**

## Follow the money

[Miami New Times reports](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/), citing the mayor's announcement, that Rockstar will pay the $3.9 million for current and additional advertising, and that the signage will be installed, maintained and removed at Rockstar's expense. The package combines a direct agreement with the county and separate ad purchases through companies that manage advertising at Miami International Airport and on county transit.

So headlines suggesting the county is *spending* $3.9 million on GTA 6 have it backwards. This is, on the county's account, money coming in. Coverage says it could help fund a long-promised waterfront park behind Kaseya Center, though [no schedule or final amount for the park has been published](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/).

## What the campaign includes

Reports describe a sprawling, roughly six-week takeover:

- The rooftop **"Welcome to Vice City"** display at Kaseya Center
- Themed lighting at **PortMiami and Miami International Airport**
- Airport walkway signage and **traffic signal box wraps**
- Separate promotional deals with the cities of Miami and Miami Beach
- Rockstar-commissioned murals, including in Wynwood
- **Black-and-pink "Vice City" jerseys** worn by the Miami Heat on November 18
- Nightclub E11EVEN Miami becoming **NINE1NINE**, its in-game counterpart, for a [November 20 launch party](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/gta-6s-3-9-million-192700636.html)

## The objections

**Miami-Dade Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz** has opposed using public resources to support a game that rewards crime, [according to reports](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/gta-6s-3-9-million-192700636.html). **Miami Beach Commissioner David Suarez** told the Associated Press he could not in good conscience tie his city's brand to it. Some county commissioners also [complained they were kept in the dark](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/) and asked why the deal didn't come to them for approval.

Even without a direct cash cost, "public resources" isn't an empty phrase. Airports, seaports, transit and an arena are public assets. Critics are asking whether those spaces should carry this particular brand at all.

## The defense

**Miami Beach Commissioner Monica Matteo-Salinas** argued that players can tell fiction from reality and that a six-week campaign isn't a permanent endorsement. **Mayor Levine Cava** has framed the deals as a way to [turn global attention into local revenue](https://nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/grand-theft-auto-six-release-rockstar-games-what-to-know-rcna609644).

Bloomberg has also [examined the takeover and the backlash](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-11-17/gta-6-s-vice-city-takeover-of-miami-draws-backlash-ahead-of-release) in a feature this week.

## Why this fight was inevitable

Miami has spent decades with a complicated relationship to its pop-culture image, from *Scarface* to *Miami Vice*. Those works sold the city to the world while dramatizing its worst stereotypes. GTA 6 is the biggest version of that bargain yet: a parody of Florida seen by tens of millions of people, now invited onto the actual skyline.
