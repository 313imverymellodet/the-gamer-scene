**Bandai Namco** is closing **Reflector Entertainment**, the Montreal studio behind *Unknown 9: Awakening*.

According to [Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2026/11/bandai-namco-to-shut-down-reflector-entertainment), Bandai Namco's board passed a resolution to **dissolve and liquidate** the studio, which is a consolidated subsidiary. The company expects to take an **extraordinary loss of about €21 million**, starting in the third quarter of its financial year ending in March 2027.

## Why Bandai Namco is closing it

In its announcement, as quoted by Gematsu, Bandai Namco said it continuously reviews its business portfolio. It considered Reflector's **future business plans, the additional investment it would need, and its chances of becoming profitable**, then decided to discontinue the business.

In plain English: the studio wasn't making money, and Bandai Namco didn't think more investment would change that.

There's no firm timeline yet. Bandai Namco says the liquidation will finish once local legal procedures are complete, and a schedule hasn't been decided. It also hasn't said how many people work at the studio.

## The story of Reflector

Reflector was founded with big ambitions. **Unknown 9** wasn't meant to be a single game. It was planned as a multimedia universe, with novels, comics, podcasts and other projects around it.

- **September 2020:** Bandai Namco bought the studio.
- **October 18, 2024:** *Unknown 9: Awakening* launched on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PS4, Xbox One and PC. Gematsu describes its reviews as mostly weak.
- **November 2024:** About a month after launch, the studio made major layoffs, [PC Gamer reported](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/unknown-9-awakening-studio-makes-major-layoffs-just-a-month-after-the-games-release/).
- **Early 2025:** Reflector's leadership said the game had failed commercially, cancelled a follow-up project and cut more staff, according to [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/action-adventure-game-unknown-9-awakening-was-a-failure-says-developer-which-has-cancelled-a-follow-up-project-and-laid-off-staff).

The studio was later expected to work on an existing Bandai Namco series instead, but according to Gematsu, those plans never materialized.

## What happens to Unknown 9?

Bandai Namco hasn't said what happens to the game or the wider Unknown 9 brand. Here's what we know, and what's still unclear:

- **The game should stay playable.** *Unknown 9: Awakening* is a single-player game, so owners don't depend on online servers to play it.
- **Store availability is unclear.** Bandai Namco hasn't said whether the game will stay on sale. If you've been curious about it, don't assume it will be around forever.
- **No sequel is coming.** The follow-up project was cancelled in early 2025, before this closure.
- **The rights stay with Bandai Namco.** Closing the studio doesn't mean the company gives up the brand, but there's no sign of plans to use it.

If anything changes, we'll update this article.

## Why it matters

Reflector is a familiar kind of casualty: a studio built around a brand-new franchise, with one game to prove itself. When that game underperformed, there was no older hit to fall back on.

It's also part of a hard month for game workers. Take-Two's 2K label reportedly cut a [third of Firaxis](/news/2026-11-23-firaxis-layoffs-third-of-staff), Ubisoft cut [166 jobs in Quebec](/news/2026-11-23-ubisoft-166-layoffs-quebec-sales-fall), and PlayStation [cancelled Fairgames](/news/2026-11-28-sony-cancels-fairgames-haven), although Sony says Haven's staff keep their jobs.

New ideas are risky by definition. When publishers close studios after a single miss, they make it less likely the next team gets the chance to build something original.
