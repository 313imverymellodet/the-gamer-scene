Tokyo Game Show turned 30 this year, and it tried something new to celebrate: a **five-day run**, the first in the show's history. Weather had other plans.

TGS 2026 was scheduled for **September 17–21** at Makuhari Messe. On September 19, organizers canceled the fifth and final day — **Monday, September 21** — as **Typhoon Dujuan** approached Japan. Ticket holders for that day were refunded. The show still ran on September 20, with a warning that some content could be changed or canceled depending on conditions.

Forecasts did not have the storm hitting the Tokyo area directly, but organizers cited strengthening wind and rain and the risk of transportation disruptions. It was the right call. Makuhari Messe is a long train ride from central Tokyo, and stranding tens of thousands of attendees is not a risk worth taking for one more day of demos.

The announcements, though, all made it out. Here is what mattered.

## Kojima: Bill Skarsgård is the face of Physint

The biggest headline came from **Hideo Kojima**, who appeared during Xbox's TGS broadcast to confirm that **Bill Skarsgård** — best known as Pennywise in *It* — will play the lead in **Physint**, Kojima Productions' action-espionage game.

The appearance was significant for another reason. Physint is now being made with **Xbox's support** after PlayStation stepped away from the project, and this was Kojima's first major update on the game since that switch. Kojima shared a new poster, shot himself, featuring Skarsgård alongside **Charlee Fraser**.

Kojima Productions describes Physint as its third original IP, and Kojima has framed it as the culmination of his 40-year career. There is still no release window, and nobody should expect one soon.

Kojima also gave an update on **OD**, his horror project with Jordan Peele, sharing behind-the-scenes footage of **Sophia Lillis** and **Hunter Schafer** in a performance capture session.

## Xbox's showcase: 17 reveals and a Call of Duty date check

Xbox used TGS for a broadcast with **17 reveals and updates**, including a surprise release and a world premiere. Two dates stood out:

- **Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4** — **October 23** on Xbox Series X|S, PS5, PC and Switch 2
- **Hope in the City** — **November 10** on Xbox Series X|S, PC and Game Pass

Xbox has now used TGS as a serious showcase for several years running, and the Kojima segment arguably made this its strongest Japanese showing yet. For a platform that has struggled in Japan for two decades, having one of the country's most famous developers on its stage is a statement.

## Bandai Namco: Ace Combat 8 and Attack on Titan 3

**Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve** was playable throughout the show ahead of its **October 2** launch on PS5, Xbox Series X|S and PC. It is one of the first releases in what is shaping up to be [a very crowded October](/news/2026-09-30-october-2026-game-releases).

Bandai Namco also dated **Attack on Titan 3** for **December 10, 2026** on PS5, Switch 2, Xbox Series X|S and PC — a smart slot three weeks after GTA 6, when players start looking for something new.

## TGS in context

TGS closed out a very busy September of showcases. **PlayStation's State of Play** on September 3 dated Final Fantasy VII Revelation for April 2027, and the **Nintendo Direct** on September 9 revealed Metroid Ravenous and a 3D open-world Kirby. We rounded those up in [our September showcase recap](/news/2026-09-30-september-2026-showcases-recap).

## The Gamer Scene take

A shortened show still delivered. The Skarsgård casting gives Physint a public face for the first time, and Kojima standing on an Xbox stage in Tokyo is the image that will outlast the typhoon headlines.

The bigger lesson is logistical. A five-day TGS was an ambitious bet on a growing audience, and it ran straight into peak typhoon season. Expect organizers to think carefully about late-September dates — or at least about contingency plans — before trying five days again.
