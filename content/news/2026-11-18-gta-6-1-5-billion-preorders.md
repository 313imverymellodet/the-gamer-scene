**Grand Theft Auto VI** may be the first entertainment product to make more than a billion dollars before most of its audience could use it. Market intelligence firm **Sensor Tower estimates the game had sold nearly 16 million copies, generating more than $1.5 billion, as of Tuesday**, according to [NBC News](https://nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/grand-theft-auto-six-release-rockstar-games-what-to-know-rcna609644) and [Australian Associated Press](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/nz-and-aussie-gamers-among-first-to-play-gta-vi).

The important caveat: **these are third-party estimates, not figures from Rockstar or Take-Two.**

## How fast the number moved

The growth over the past week is striking, if the estimates are comparable. Around Take-Two's earnings report on November 9, one [Sensor Tower estimate cited in coverage](https://www.quantisnow.com/articles/take-two-jumps-8-on-gta-vi-preorders-after-an-18-fall-in-bookings) put digital preorders at about 7.71 million units and $751 million. By this Tuesday, the headline estimate had roughly doubled.

Be careful comparing those directly. Estimates can differ in what they include, and late-window preorders and boxed sales may be counted differently. The direction is clear. The exact curve isn't.

In July, you may remember, a viral claim said GTA 6 had already made $1 billion. That was false at the time, and [Newzoo's estimate was about $260 million](/news/2026-07-17-gta-6-preorder-record-newzoo-estimate) for the first week. The $1 billion mark appears to have been passed only recently.

## What Take-Two actually says

Take-Two won't give a number. On its November earnings call, CEO Strauss Zelnick said preorders were [running far above the company's initial expectations](https://www.gameshub.com/news/article/gta-6-pre-orders-zelnick-earnings-call-2929876/), and the company described them as reaching unprecedented levels. It also [confirmed development of the game was complete](https://outputlag.com/news/gta-6-officially-goes-gold-take-two-pre-orders-unprecedented/).

Wall Street noticed. Take-Two's shares [jumped about 8% after the report](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/10/take-two-jumps-8-as-gta-vi-preorders-run-far-in-excess-of-expectations-sony-and-roblox-slide-2/), and the rally came even though the company's second-quarter net bookings missed its own forecast. For investors, GTA 6 was the only line that mattered.

## The scale of the bet

NBC reports that development has reportedly cost more than $1 billion, with a team of nearly 5,000 people, citing The Guardian. If the Sensor Tower estimate is close, gross preorder spending has already passed that reported budget. That isn't the same as profit: platform fees, retailer cuts, marketing and taxes all come out before Take-Two sees its share.

## Why the $100 edition matters

Earlier this year, Sensor Tower estimated that about [89% of early preorders were for the $99.99 Ultimate Edition](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-news-roundup). Take-Two's CEO said only that early buyers skewed premium, and cautioned that the most dedicated fans buy first. As casual players arrive, the average price per copy will probably fall.

If you're still deciding, here's our [Standard vs Ultimate breakdown](/news/2026-11-15-gta-6-standard-vs-ultimate-worth-it).

## What to watch next

The first official sales figure is the one that counts. Rockstar and Take-Two have a history of announcing big launch milestones, and one early statement could reset every estimate above. Analysts will also be comparing results with the expectations built into Take-Two's full-year guidance, which one report said [was raised for the December quarter](https://gamesbeat.com/all-the-takeaways-on-gta-from-the-take-two-earnings-call-strauss-zelnick/).

Until then, treat every figure as a well-informed estimate. Different trackers measure different things, and none of them has access to Rockstar's full sales data. It's still a very large estimate.
