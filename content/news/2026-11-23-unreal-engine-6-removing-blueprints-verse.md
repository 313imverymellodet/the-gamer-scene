Epic Games is making the biggest change to Unreal Engine in years, and many of the people who use it are not happy.

At **Unreal Fest Tokyo** on November 3, Epic revealed that **Unreal Engine 6** will remove **Blueprints**, **Actors** and the **C++ Gameplay Framework**, according to [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28098/epic-games-will-remove-blueprints-and-actors-from-unreal-engine-6), citing Japanese outlet Game Makers. Game logic will move to **Verse**, the programming language Epic first introduced for Fortnite creators.

The reaction has been loud for most of November. A [Change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/keep-blueprint-scripting-support-in-unreal-engine-6) asks Epic to keep Blueprint support, and a thread on Epic's own forums titled ["They want to remove Blueprint in UE6"](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/they-want-to-remove-blueprint-in-ue6/2729352) has run to well over a dozen pages.

## What are Blueprints, and why do they matter?

If you've never made a game, here's the short version. **Blueprints** are Unreal's visual scripting system. Instead of writing code, you connect boxes and wires on screen to make things happen: open a door, start a cutscene, spawn an enemy.

They're a big reason Unreal became so popular. Blueprints let artists, designers, students and solo developers build working games without being expert programmers, and they're used in large studios too. **Actors** are the basic objects placed in an Unreal level. Together, they're how a huge number of Unreal games are built.

## What's changing in Unreal Engine 6

According to Inven Global's report on the keynote:

- Epic's slides marked Blueprints, Actors and the C++ Gameplay Framework as **"Remove,"** not simply deprecated.
- Game logic moves to **Verse**.
- Levels will use Epic's **Scene Graph**, built from "entities" made of components rather than Actors.
- **C++ stays** at the engine's lower levels, such as rendering and physics.
- Epic plans **AI-assisted tools** to help convert Blueprint and C++ gameplay code to Verse, though their format hasn't been decided.

Tim Sweeney reportedly called it a painful change and apologized to the community. Inven Global also reports that he linked the decision to AI, which he said had made Epic's internal teams significantly more productive. Asked on X which AI models know Verse, Sweeney said they had seen little of it in training, but that this would change by January.

## What's the timeline?

This isn't happening tomorrow. According to Inven Global, **UE6 Early Access is planned for the end of 2027**.

It's also a reversal. At Unreal Fest Chicago in June, Epic had said Actors and Blueprints would stay in the first version of UE6 and be phased out over time. Now they're expected to be gone from the start of Early Access.

**Projects already in development on Unreal Engine 5 can stay on UE5.** That's important. Most games you'll play in the next few years are being built on UE5 and won't be forced to switch.

## Why developers are pushing back

The complaints fall into a few groups:

- **Accessibility.** Blueprints are how many people learn game development, from students to artists to hobbyists. Replacing them with a text programming language raises the barrier to entry.
- **Maturity.** Critics on Epic's forums argue Verse's tools still don't come close to what Blueprints can do.
- **Trust.** Epic said one thing in June and another in November. Studios plan engine choices years ahead, and sudden changes make that harder.
- **Control.** Moving game logic to a language Epic owns ties developers more tightly to Epic's ecosystem.

Supporters argue that a modern, unified language could make Unreal faster, cleaner and easier to build tools for, and that Verse has already powered a huge amount of content in Fortnite.

## What it means for players

In the short term, nothing. Games coming out in 2026, 2027 and probably 2028 are on Unreal Engine 5 or older engines.

In the long term, it matters more. Unreal powers a large share of games, from giant AAA releases to small indies. If UE6 makes it harder for small teams and newcomers to build things, that could change who makes Unreal games. If the AI tools and Verse deliver what Epic promises, it could do the opposite. We won't know which until developers actually get their hands on it in late 2027.
