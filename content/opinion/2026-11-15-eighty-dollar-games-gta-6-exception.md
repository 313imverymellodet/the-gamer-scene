Grand Theft Auto VI costs **$79.99** for the Standard Edition and **$99.99** for the Ultimate. GTA V launched at $59.99 in 2013. That's a $20 increase on the base game, and most of the internet seems to have shrugged. Early preorders reportedly skewed heavily toward the *more* expensive edition.

I'm not here to tell you GTA 6 isn't worth $80. It might be. I'm here to say it shouldn't become everyone else's excuse.

## Why GTA 6 might earn it

Be honest about what Rockstar is shipping. This is a game more than a decade in development, built by one of the largest teams in entertainment, with a fully realized state instead of a single city, two protagonists, a famously dense world and the production values of a blockbuster film. GTA V gave people hundreds of hours for $60. If GTA 6 does anything close to that, the cost per hour is still absurdly low.

Thirteen years of inflation also matters. $59.99 in 2013 isn't $59.99 today. By that math, $80 isn't a shocking increase.

## The problem: the precedent

The danger isn't GTA 6's price. It's what every other publisher does with it.

The moment the biggest game ever made launches at $80 and sells like nothing else in history, every pitch deck in the industry gets a new slide: *"Consumers have shown they'll pay $80."* And then a twelve-hour licensed action game shows up at $80. A yearly sports release shows up at $80. A live-service game with a battle pass shows up at $80.

Those games aren't GTA 6. They didn't take thirteen years, they don't have Rockstar's scale, and many of them already charge for things on top of the base price.

## Price should follow value, not ambition

This site has argued before that runtime isn't value. Earlier this year Mixtape, a four-hour game, became the year's best-reviewed release at $19.99. Value comes from quality, care and how a game respects your time and money. Price should be a consequence of that, not a statement of how important a publisher thinks its game is.

GTA 6 has a real claim to premium pricing. Most $70 games already don't.

## The editions problem

There's another trend worth watching. GTA 6's $99.99 Ultimate Edition reportedly drew most early preorders. That tells publishers something too: the base price isn't the ceiling, it's the floor, and a big chunk of the audience will pay for the top tier without blinking. Expect more "real" content to drift into premium editions across the industry if that number holds. That's the version of this precedent I worry about most.

## What I want from players

Buy GTA 6 if you want it. It's very likely to be the event of the decade. But when the next game asks for $80, ask the same questions you'd ask of Rockstar:

- How long was this in development, and does it show?
- How much content is actually here?
- What am I being charged for on top of the base price?
- Would this game be just as good at $60?

If the answers don't hold up, don't pay it. Prices are set by what we accept.

## The bottom line

Rockstar may well earn every dollar of $79.99. Everyone else should have to earn it too.
