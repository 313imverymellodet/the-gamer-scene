Right on schedule, the panic has arrived.

On **Fox & Friends**, host Rachel Campos-Duffy [said she doesn't see GTA 6 as good for our culture](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/fox-news-grand-theft-auto-vi-backlash_n_6afd0851e4b0e902c23c2c90), and reportedly referred to protagonist Lucia as a "hooker" before being corrected. In Miami, [officials are objecting to Vice City branding](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-miami-vice-city-takeover-backlash) because the game rewards crime. In South Korea, [regulators nearly refused it altogether](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-south-korea-rating-nudity).

Every few years, Grand Theft Auto releases and adults who've never played it discover that it's about crime. We've been doing this since before many of its players were born.

## Getting the basics wrong is not a small thing

I'll start with the Lucia moment, because it reveals the whole problem. Rockstar's most visible, heavily marketed lead is a woman who [learned to fight from her father, went to prison, and is trying to change her family's odds](/news/2026-11-15-gta-6-jason-lucia-explained). That information is on the official website. It has been in the trailers for years.

If your argument is that a piece of culture is damaging society, the minimum entry fee is knowing what's in it. Reaching for a sexist assumption about the first woman to lead a modern GTA doesn't suggest close study. It suggests a conclusion in search of a segment.

## Here's what the panic never reckons with

The fear has been the same for three decades: play violent games, become violent. Researchers have argued about aggression effects in the lab for a long time, and the debate isn't completely settled. But the sweeping version, in which games like this turn players into criminals, has never been established. Hundreds of millions of copies of Grand Theft Auto games have been sold. If the panic had been right, we would all know it by now.

GTA V is one of the best-selling entertainment products in history. People played it, had a laugh at Los Santos, and went to work.

## And yet the critics aren't entirely wrong

Here's where I'll lose some of you. Being sick of moral panics shouldn't mean pretending GTA is above criticism.

This series has a real history worth arguing about: its treatment of women, its reliance on shock, the way its satire sometimes becomes the thing it claims to mock. GTA V's torture mission deserved every bit of scrutiny it received. And GTA 6 has [nudity, drug use and violence](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-south-korea-rating-nudity) that are absolutely not for children. Parents who take that seriously aren't hysterical. They're doing their job. If a 12-year-old is playing this game, the failure isn't Rockstar's, but it is a failure.

Even early reviews aren't uniformly impressed with its social commentary. [Ars Technica found some of the jokes stale](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/pop-culture/2026/11/18/gta-6-reviews-metacritic/). That's a much more interesting conversation than whether a video game will end civilization.

## What a smart critique would sound like

A serious critic would ask whether GTA 6's satire has anything new to say about America. They'd ask how it handles class, race, immigration, policing and the Florida it parodies. They'd ask what it means that a $1 billion corporate product is selling us rebellion. They might even ask about [the workers Rockstar fired](/news/2026-11-18-rockstar-union-tribunal-ends-gta-6-launch).

Notice how none of those require you to believe players can't tell a game from reality. They do require you to look at the game.

## The free publicity machine

There's one more irony. Rockstar has benefited from this cycle since the 1990s. Outrage is marketing. Every furious segment tells a teenager that this is the forbidden thing and tells an adult that it's a cultural event. With an estimated [$1.5 billion in presales](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders), it's safe to say the warnings aren't working.

## The bottom line

By all means, criticize Grand Theft Auto VI. It's big enough to take it. Just play it first, or at the very least, learn the name and story of the woman on the box.
