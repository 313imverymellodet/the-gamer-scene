Here's how one week went at the company that owns Grand Theft Auto.

On **Tuesday, November 17**, Take-Two's 2K label laid off around **60 people** at Firaxis, about **a third of the studio** behind Civilization and XCOM, [according to Bloomberg](/news/2026-11-23-firaxis-layoffs-third-of-staff). The studio head left too. 2K called it "restructuring and recentering."

On **Thursday**, Grand Theft Auto VI launched.

On **Friday**, Rockstar announced [$3.79 billion in day-one retail sales](/news/2026-11-23-gta-6-sales-3-79-billion-biggest-launch-ever), the biggest launch of any entertainment product ever.

If you work in games, you already know how both of those things can be true. If you don't, it probably looks insane. I think the second reaction is the healthier one.

## The defense, stated fairly

The business case is easy to make, so let's make it properly.

Take-Two runs its labels separately. Rockstar's success doesn't automatically cover losses somewhere else. Civilization VII had a rough reception and, by Take-Two's own account, slow sales. Firaxis was reportedly working on an XCOM game that missed an important milestone. Studios that don't deliver get restructured, and the $3.79 billion figure is retail spending, not Take-Two's profit. Platforms and retailers take their share first.

All of that is reasonable. None of it is the point.

## Why the timing still matters

Layoffs are not weather. Somebody chooses when they happen.

Take-Two knew exactly when GTA 6 was coming out. It had spent more than a year telling investors the game was on track. It chose to cut a third of one of its most respected studios **48 hours** before its biggest-ever release, when the industry's attention would be somewhere else entirely.

Maybe that was deliberate. Maybe it wasn't. Either way, the message to every developer at every Take-Two studio is the same: **record revenue at the top does not protect you.** If a historic, $3.79 billion launch week doesn't change the math, nothing will.

And this isn't a one-off. The same month, the UK tribunal over [34 workers Rockstar dismissed](/news/2026-11-18-rockstar-union-tribunal-ends-gta-6-launch) ended without a verdict as the game shipped. In Canada, Ubisoft cut [166 jobs in Quebec](/news/2026-11-23-ubisoft-166-layoffs-quebec-sales-fall) the same day GTA 6 launched.

## The real problem is the model

The uncomfortable truth is that GTA 6's success is part of what makes cuts like this easier, not harder.

The modern AAA industry is built on a handful of enormous bets. When one pays off, it proves the model: spend years and a reported billion dollars on a single game, and it can earn more on day one than any movie ever has. Everything that isn't that kind of bet starts to look like a cost to manage. A strategy studio that makes beloved games for a smaller audience is exactly the kind of team that loses in that comparison.

That's bad for players, too. The games that made many of us love this medium weren't all billion-dollar blockbusters. XCOM, Civilization and games like them come from teams given time to build something specific and strange. An industry that only protects its biggest bets will eventually only make its biggest bets.

## What players can actually do

I'm not telling you not to play GTA 6. [I've said before](/opinion/2026-11-18-play-gta-6-care-about-fired-workers) that you can love the game and still care about the people who make games. Most of the workers in these stories aren't asking for a boycott.

What you can do is pay attention. Notice when companies announce cuts during their biggest weeks. Support the studios and games you want to keep existing, especially the mid-sized ones. Don't let "record launch" be the only headline anyone remembers from this week.

Because the record will be broken one day. The 60 people at Firaxis still lost their jobs.
