**Grand Theft Auto VI** [received eight Game Awards nominations on Tuesday](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-game-awards-eight-nominations), including Game of the Year. It wasn't out yet. In most of the world, it still isn't.

Before anyone sends the angry replies: I think GTA 6 probably deserves to be there. It has a [99 on Metacritic](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-reviews-metacritic-99-roundup). If it wins Game of the Year, I doubt it'll be a scandal. My problem isn't the game. It's the calendar.

## The rule is technically fine and practically absurd

The Game Awards' cutoff says a title must be [commercially available by November 20](https://gamingbolt.com/gta-6-is-officially-eligible-for-the-game-awards-2026-barely-making-the-cutoff-date). GTA 6 launches November 19, so it qualifies. Nobody broke anything.

But notice what that means. The nominees were announced on November 17, *before* the eligibility deadline had even passed. So the shortlist was finalized while the most important contender wasn't available to the public, and jurors were relying on review access or on outlets' early verdicts.

Public voting [ends December 9](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2026/11/the-game-awards-2026-nominees-to-be-announced-next-week-zelda-and-gta-6-eligible). The ceremony is December 10. GTA 6's missions alone are [estimated at around 50 hours](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-how-long-to-beat), and much longer if you do what the game seems designed for. Most fans voting for it won't have finished it. Lots of them won't have started.

They'll vote anyway. That's the whole issue.

## Awards should reward what a game is, not how big it is

Consider what you're asking people to judge. Best Narrative, for a story most voters haven't completed. Best Performance, for a character arc they haven't seen conclude. Game of the Year, for something they've known for three weeks, set against games people have lived with since February.

A game released in spring gets months of scrutiny. Its bugs have been discovered, its ending argued over, its reputation tested. A game released in the final hours of eligibility gets the most valuable thing in awards season: pure momentum. Hype has not yet had time to meet reality.

This isn't only a GTA issue. Late-year launches have enjoyed that recency advantage for a long time. GTA 6 simply makes it impossible to ignore.

## The games that pay the price

The real victims aren't Rockstar's competitors at the top. *Ocarina of Time*, *Resident Evil Requiem* and *007 First Light* will be fine. It's the smaller, stranger games that fight for a sliver of attention during the one night the whole industry is watching. When the conversation becomes a referendum on a billion-dollar juggernaut, everyone else is background.

## The obvious fix

Move the cutoff earlier. Set it at October 31, or require a game to have been available for at least 30 days before nominations close. Anything released later rolls into next year's show, where it can be judged with a full year of perspective.

Yes, that would have meant GTA 6 competing at the 2027 awards. So what? Do you really think anyone would forget it? It would've been the overwhelming favorite twelve months later, and every vote would actually have meant something.

The film world has had versions of this argument for decades. Nobody seriously suggests that a movie should be nominated before audiences have had any opportunity to see it.

## The bottom line

The Game Awards wanted the biggest game in the world on its stage, and of course it did. It's great television. But an award is supposed to be a judgment, and you can't really judge what you haven't played.

Give it the trophy next year if it earns it. It'll still be there.
