For six years, the Xbox Series S has been the console people bought for sensible reasons. It was affordable. It was small. It was a great way into Game Pass. And for six years, developers have muttered that its limited memory was making their jobs harder.

Grand Theft Auto VI may be the moment that argument ended.

## What the numbers show

On Series S, GTA 6 [starts from a 720p internal resolution and uses AMD's FSR 4 to reach 1080p](https://www.purexbox.com/news/2026/11/gta-6-comparison-shows-difference-between-xbox-series-x-and-series-s), with reduced ray tracing and textures. Reports on Digital Foundry's testing describe [frequent drops below 30fps](https://windowsforum.com/news/gta-6-xbox-series-s-performance-digital-foundry-finds-frequent-sub-30-fps-drops.453047/), with busy scenes in the mid-20s and the heaviest moments sinking lower.

Digital Foundry's advice, as reported by several outlets, was blunt: if you can, consider another console for this game, at least until performance improves.

The Series X, by contrast, [runs at an internal 1440p with a 30fps target](https://twistedvoxel.com/gta-6-xbox-series-x-vs-series-s-performance/), much closer to PS5.

## Credit where it's due

I want to be clear about something. Getting a game like this running on that hardware at all is a remarkable piece of engineering. Rockstar could have shipped something far worse. Experts [have praised the work](https://www.levelup.com/en/news/does-gta-6-run-well-on-xbox-series-s-experts-praise-rockstars-work-but-issue-an-important-warning-about-microsofts-console/) even while warning buyers. Plenty of people will play it on Series S and have a wonderful time, particularly on a smaller TV.

My problem isn't with Rockstar, and it certainly isn't with anybody who owns the console.

## The promise was the problem

The Series S was sold as a next-generation console at an entry-level price: same games, lower resolution. That was the deal. In the real world, "lower resolution" quietly became "lower resolution, and also worse frame rates, and also fewer effects."

A frame rate that already targets 30 has no room to fall. At 30, a drop is something you feel. When a console's weakest moments land in the 20s during chases and gunfights, that isn't a cosmetic compromise anymore. It changes how the game plays.

The suspected culprit, according to Digital Foundry's reported analysis, may be the Series S's 10 GB memory configuration, although that hasn't been confirmed. If it's true, it's the exact concern developers have raised since 2020.

## Who should feel worst about this?

Not the kid who got a Series S for Christmas because a family could afford that and not a Series X. Those buyers are who this console was for. They're the ones getting the roughest version of the year's biggest game, at the same **$79.99** everybody else pays.

That's the part I can't get past. The cheapest console gets the weakest experience, as you'd expect, but the game doesn't get any cheaper.

## What Microsoft should do

Patches may help, and Rockstar has every reason to keep improving this version. But Microsoft should also be straightforward with buyers this holiday. If a store is selling a Series S bundle on the promise of GTA 6, shoppers deserve to know what they're getting. "Plays GTA 6" is technically true. It's not the whole truth.

## What you should do

If you already own one, play it. A 120Hz output with VRR on a compatible TV may help smooth things out. If you're buying a console primarily for GTA 6, though, don't pick the Series S to save money without seeing it in motion first.

## The bottom line

The Series S had a good run as the people's console. But every generation eventually produces the game that shows you where the floor is. This one has arrived, and it's standing on it.
