This week, **Grand Theft Auto VI** will be called the greatest game ever made by a lot of people, some of them with good reason. It has a [99 on Metacritic](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-reviews-metacritic-99-roundup) and an estimated [$1.5 billion in presales](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders).

Also this week, [the tribunal over 34 dismissed Rockstar workers finished in Glasgow](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/rockstars-union-busting-tribunal-comes-to-an-end-pending-verdict/) with no verdict yet. They can't go back to work while they wait.

Both of those things are true at once. I think we can handle that.

## What's actually in dispute

To be fair, I don't know who wins this case, and neither do you. The Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain says the firings were union busting. **Rockstar denies that**, and says the workers were dismissed for leaking confidential information outside the company. A tribunal [earlier declined to grant them interim relief](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/new-ruling-in-rockstar-games-legal-battle-means-the-gta-6-studio-wont-be-forced-to-pay-fired-employees-interim-relief-we-regret-that-we-were-put-in-a-position-where-dismissals-were-necessary). A judge will decide the main question on the evidence.

Rockstar also has a legitimate reason to care deeply about leaks. It's been [hacked](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-hacker-arion-kurtaj-guilty-plea) and targeted by [an ongoing leaker](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-leak-crisis-cyberleek). No studio wants its most valuable secret discussed loosely.

So I'm not here to declare a verdict. I'm here to argue that you shouldn't need one in order to pay attention.

## The workers made this easy for you

Here's the remarkable part. As [we reported in August](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-news-roundup), the dismissed staff have asked fans **not** to boycott the game. They helped make it. They want you to see it.

That removes the excuse most of us reach for. Usually these conversations become a purity test: buy the product and you're complicit, skip it or you're a hypocrite. So people tune out. These workers have declined to make that demand. All they've asked is that the spotlight on their work also fall on how they were treated.

That's about the most reasonable request anybody will make of you during this entire launch.

## Greatness has a cost, and we love not hearing about it

We've all learned the rhythm by now. A huge game ships. Everyone marvels at the detail: every storefront, every reflection, every background conversation. Then, usually later, we hear about the hours, the stress, the people who burned out.

Reports describe a production of [nearly 5,000 people and more than $1 billion](https://nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/grand-theft-auto-six-release-rockstar-games-what-to-know-rcna609644). When you drive through Vice City and notice something tiny and perfect, somebody spent real days of their life on it. Admiring that work and asking about those people aren't opposing impulses. They're the same impulse. If the detail moves you, the people behind it should matter too.

## The uncomfortable bit for fans

Be honest about how this usually goes. If a beloved company is accused of something, a section of its fans treats the accusation as an attack on themselves. Expect comments under this column telling me to shut up and play the game.

I'd ask those people one question: what would you lose by wanting a fair outcome? Your copy of GTA 6 will work exactly the same. Rockstar's reputation can survive a tribunal. And if the company did nothing wrong, the process will say so.

## What caring actually looks like

It's not complicated:

- Follow the verdict when it lands, even after the launch hype fades
- Read what both sides say
- Don't harass Rockstar employees, who have nothing to do with this decision
- If you want to support the workers, the union's campaign is public

## The bottom line

Play the game. Love the game, if it earns it. Just don't let the biggest launch in history turn the people who helped build it into a footnote.
