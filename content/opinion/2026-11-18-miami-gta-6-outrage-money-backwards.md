To hear some of the coverage tell it, Miami-Dade County is spending millions of dollars of public money to promote a video game about crime. It makes for a great outrage headline. It also appears to be wrong.

According to [the mayor's announcement, as reported by Miami New Times](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/), **Rockstar is paying the $3.9 million**, and its money covers installing, maintaining and removing the signage. That doesn't end the debate. It does change what the debate is about.

## This is a company buying ads

Strip away the drama and here's what happened: a very rich company wants to advertise a product, and a local government has airport, port, transit and arena space to rent. Governments do this constantly. Airports are covered in ads for banks, casinos, liquor brands and streaming shows, some of them for very violent television.

If Miami-Dade gets $3.9 million from Rockstar, and some of that really ends up supporting [the long-promised waterfront park](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/), then a parody of Florida will have paid for a little bit of actual Florida. I find that kind of perfect.

## The critics do have a point. Just a different one.

Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz and Commissioner David Suarez aren't foolish for objecting. There is a real difference between a private billboard and a government building carrying a message. When the words "Welcome to Vice City" sit on a county-owned arena, people can fairly ask what the county is endorsing.

There's also a process complaint that I think is the strongest argument of all. [Some commissioners say they were kept in the dark](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/news/miami-dade-inks-3-9-million-gta-vi-ad-deal-promises-park-funding-40594584/) and never got to approve the deal. If you're going to turn public landmarks into a giant ad for an adults-only crime game, elected officials probably should get a say. And residents deserve a clear accounting of where the money goes, especially since no park timeline or final figure has been published.

So argue about transparency. Argue about whether civic spaces should be for sale. Those are serious questions. But "taxpayers are funding GTA" isn't the argument the facts support.

## Miami built this brand long before Rockstar showed up

Here's what makes the moral objection hard to take entirely seriously. Miami has been cashing in on its own glamorous, dangerous mythology for generations. *Scarface* and *Miami Vice* are woven into how the world sees the city, and the tourism economy has benefited from that aura of neon, money and excess.

GTA's Vice City didn't invent that image. It satirized it. Now the satire has come home with a checkbook, and some officials are shocked to find it on the skyline.

And consider what's actually being advertised. [Critics have called this the most mature GTA yet](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-reviews-metacritic-99-roundup), with a story about two people trying to survive a stacked deck. It's still a crime game, and it's still rated for adults. No one needs to pretend it's a civics lesson. But "rewards crime" is a pretty thin summary of a medium that has been exploring crime as long as movies and novels have.

## What Miami should do now

Publish the contracts in plain language. Say exactly how much reaches the county and where it goes. Put future deals like this in front of commissioners. And if the park gets funded, build the thing.

Oh, and maybe name a bench after Tommy Vercetti.

## The bottom line

There's a legitimate debate about whether public space should sell itself to a game like this. There isn't much of one about who's writing the check. Miami isn't paying for Vice City. Vice City is paying Miami.
