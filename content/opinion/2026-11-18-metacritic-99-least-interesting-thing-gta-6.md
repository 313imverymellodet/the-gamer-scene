Grand Theft Auto VI has a 99 on Metacritic. It's tied with *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time* for the highest score in the site's history, and by this afternoon half the internet had decided that settles it: greatest game ever made, case closed.

It doesn't settle anything. And I say that believing GTA 6 is probably extraordinary.

## A 99 measures agreement, not greatness

Here's what the number actually means. It's a weighted average of critics who mostly gave the game top marks. If nearly everyone gives something a 9.5 or 10, you get a 99. It tells you that very few critics had a serious problem with it. It doesn't tell you that it's better than a game that scored 96 and made some people furious while changing others' lives.

Consensus and greatness are different things. Some of the most important games ever made were divisive. Metacritic rewards the absence of objections, and blockbusters with enormous budgets are built to give people very few of them.

## Look at what critics actually said

The reviews are more interesting than the average. [The Verge found the combat clunky and the weapon wheel frustrating. The Guardian said missions can fail for unclear reasons. Ars Technica found some of the satire stale and Jason less compelling than Lucia](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/pop-culture/2026/11/18/gta-6-reviews-metacritic/). The game also [runs at 30 frames per second on every console](/news/2026-11-15-gta-6-60fps-what-we-know).

Those are not small categories. Combat, mission design, writing and performance are the game. Critics can reasonably decide those issues are outweighed by everything else, and clearly most did. But "flawed masterpiece" and "99" are telling two different stories, and only one of them is useful if you're deciding how to spend $80.

## The 10-point scale is broken anyway

Game criticism has been stuck on a compressed scale for decades. A 7 is treated as a disappointment. An 8 can trigger an angry mob. With a release this big, there's enormous pressure, intended or not, to meet the moment. No outlet is eager to be the one that drags down the most anticipated game of all time, and history suggests dissenters get harassed for trying.

I'm not accusing anybody of dishonesty. I believe these critics loved the game. I'm saying the system makes unanimity more likely than it should be.

## And nobody has lived with it yet

Reviews were written from pre-release code, under deadline, by people playing at an inhuman pace. Ocarina's reputation has had nearly three decades to prove itself. It shaped how 3D games are built. GTA 6's has had about a day.

The question that matters isn't whether it scored a 99 this week. It's whether, in 2036, developers are still borrowing from it, players are still returning to it, and its world still feels astonishing. It might be. I hope so. But you can't review that into existence.

## What I'd watch instead

Forget the aggregate. Pay attention to whether Leonida's systems hold up after 100 hours, whether the story sticks the landing for ordinary players, and whether the frame rate bothers people as much as it bothers Digital Foundry. Watch the user scores with skepticism too, because they'll be flooded by fans and haters alike.

## The bottom line

GTA 6 may be one of the greatest games ever made. A number on an aggregation site can't be what proves it. Let people play the thing.
