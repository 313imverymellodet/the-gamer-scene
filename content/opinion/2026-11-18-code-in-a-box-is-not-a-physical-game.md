Let's call this what it is. If you buy **Grand Theft Auto VI** in a box, you are paying $79.99 for [a piece of plastic with a voucher inside](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-no-disc-code-in-box-explained). There's no disc. There's no game on anything you can hold. You take it home, type in a code and download up to 212 GB.

That isn't a physical game. It's a gift card with cover art.

## I understand why Rockstar did it

Rockstar has real reasons to be paranoid. It suffered [a devastating hack in 2022](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-hacker-arion-kurtaj-guilty-plea), and it spent this year fighting [the CyberLeek leaks](/news/2026-08-20-gta-6-leak-crisis-cyberleek). Discs out in the supply chain before release are a genuine spoiler risk for a game with this much story to protect. [Kotaku's reporting pointed to leak prevention](https://kotaku.com/physical-copies-of-gta-6-will-just-be-a-code-in-a-box-with-no-disc-at-launch-2000709919) as the motive.

Fine. But understanding the motive isn't the same as accepting the result. There's also an obvious business benefit that doesn't get mentioned as often: no disc means no used copy. Every person who might have borrowed a friend's game or picked up a pre-owned copy for less now has one fewer way of doing so.

## What a disc actually gives you

People who defend this tend to say physical media is dying anyway, and that discs mostly just authorize a download these days. There's some truth there. Modern games are patched constantly, and a disc from 2026 won't contain everything.

But a disc still gives you things a code never will:

- **You can sell it.** That has real value, especially for people on a budget.
- **You can lend it.** Passing a game around is one of the oldest traditions in this hobby.
- **You can give it away.** A kid can inherit it from an older sibling.
- **It reduces what you have to download** when the game is designed that way.
- **It doesn't depend entirely on a storefront** staying open and keeping the game listed.

A redeemed code gives you none of that. It's dead the moment you use it.

## The 212 GB insult

This is where it stops being abstract. Not everybody has fast, unlimited internet. A New Zealand network executive [said the file amounts to more than a quarter of a typical household's monthly data](https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/nz-and-aussie-gamers-among-first-to-play-gta-vi). Rural players, people on data caps and families sharing slow connections are exactly the audience for whom a disc could matter most.

Those players walk into a store, see a box on a shelf, and could reasonably assume they're buying a game. Sure, the packaging discloses it. Plenty of parents buying gifts this holiday still won't notice until Christmas morning.

## The precedent is the real story

Publishers watch Rockstar. If the biggest launch in history can ship a boxed code at full price and [gross an estimated $1.5 billion before release](/news/2026-11-18-gta-6-1-5-billion-preorders), then every executive with a spreadsheet has their answer. Why press discs at all? Why support a resale market you don't profit from?

And if the rumored disc does arrive later, as [Kotaku indicated it would](https://kotaku.com/physical-copies-of-gta-6-will-just-be-a-code-in-a-box-with-no-disc-at-launch-2000709919), the most loyal collectors get a lovely choice: wait, or pay twice.

## What should happen

Rockstar should announce a firm date for a real disc version and treat people who bought the box fairly when it does. Retailers should label these products in giant letters. And everyone, including outlets like this one, should stop calling them "physical copies" without an asterisk.

## The bottom line

I'll give Rockstar this: it's a beautiful box. But you don't own a game because you own its packaging. Don't let anybody tell you otherwise.
